After a Ph.D. in astrochemistry at Universit\'e Paris-Saclay, where I analysed

underpin modern deep learning frameworks.

Since then I have been conducting independent research in neural network
interpretability. I built a toolchain that opens up a 15-layer transformer
(Leela Chess Zero) and reads out what it computes internally: the model is
converted from ONNX to PyTorch, and forward hooks on every encoder layer capture
its attention during inference. What I value most is not the tooling but what it
taught me: I found two systematic errors in my own pipeline that produced
entirely plausible results --- neither raised an exception, neither failed a
test, and one had already reached a published write-up before I caught it. I
corrected it publicly:
\href{https://thejusmahajan.github.io/blog-lc0-attention-frame.html}{thejusmahajan.github.io/blog-lc0-attention-frame.html}.

That habit is what I would bring beyond the tools themselves: a model returning
confident, well-shaped, wrong answers is the failure mode that costs most and
announces itself least. My work at HealthTwiSt on a national medical registry
pipeline (143,000 patient records, some 300 clinics) rested on the same
discipline --- every refactoring step verified byte-identical before acceptance.
