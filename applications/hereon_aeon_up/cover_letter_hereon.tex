\textbf{Application as Postdoctoral Researcher --- Probabilistic Deep Learning
for Urban Air Quality (AEON-UP) --- Reference Code 1056}

\vspace{0.3em}
Dear Dr.~Ramacher,


I am writing to apply for the postdoctoral position on probabilistic deep
learning for urban air quality. What draws me to AEON-UP is the clause most
advertisements leave out: the predictions are to come \emph{with uncertainty
estimates}. That question is not an abstract interest for me.

Since finishing my post-doc I have conducted independent research in neural
network interpretability, extracting the internal representations of a 15-layer
transformer in PyTorch. In the course of it I found two systematic errors in my
own pipeline. Both produced entirely plausible output --- smooth, well-shaped
results that raised no exception and failed no test. One had already reached a
published write-up before I caught it, and I corrected it publicly:
\href{https://thejusmahajan.github.io/blog-lc0-attention-frame.html}{thejusmahajan.github.io/blog-lc0-attention-frame.html}.

The lesson is the one AEON-UP is built around. A confidently wrong model is more
dangerous than a visibly uncertain one, and where the output may inform decisions
about what people breathe, a calibrated statement of what the model does
\emph{not} know is not a refinement --- it is the product. Sparse, irregular
monitoring networks are exactly the setting where a model must interpolate and
declare how far to trust the interpolation.

I would come to this from the physical side as much as the learned one. During
my post-doc at Universit\"at Hamburg I developed a plankton life
cycle model within the ERGOM framework and ran hindcast and projection
simulations for warming scenarios, working daily with gridded spatio-temporal
output in NetCDF on Linux HPC systems. I therefore know what a physics-based
transport model is, what its output looks like at scale, and where its biases
tend to live --- which matters when the task is to couple such a model with a
learned one rather than replace it. I followed your group's chemistry transport
modelling with CMAQ over the North Sea and European domains with real interest,
and contributing on the learned side of that coupling is exactly what I want.

I should be straightforward: my experience with Bayesian deep learning and
neural processes is at the level of reading and implementation rather than
published work. I am building it deliberately --- in September I begin the
certified course ``Deployable Data Analysis \& AI Pipelines with HPC'' at the
Supercomputing-Akademie of the HLRS, following earlier HPC training at the
J\"ulich Supercomputing Centre. What I bring meanwhile is ten years of building
computational models and interrogating whether their output can be trusted ---
and a demonstrated willingness to find that the answer is no.

Hereon is not unfamiliar to me: I worked at the centre as a guest scientist in
2025 on ecosystem modelling, and would be glad to return. I hold work
authorisation for Germany, live in Hamburg, and am available for the October
start. I would welcome the chance to discuss the project.
